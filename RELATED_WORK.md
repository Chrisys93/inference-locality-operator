# Related work: reuse units, locality and correctness in multi-replica LLM serving

Scan date: 2026-11-26. Scope: 2023-2026, weighted to 2025-2026. Every entry below was either opened (abstract/proceedings/docs page) or seen as a search result; the "Checked" column says which. Numbers are the authors' own, copied from abstracts or pages; none were reproduced here.

## Gap

What exists. (1) Routers and schedulers that trade prefix-cache locality against load across replicas are mature: llm-d precise prefix-cache scoring from vLLM KV events, the SGLang cache-aware router, NVIDIA Dynamo's KV router, Preble, DLPM/D²LPM, DualMap, CacheRoute and an ICLR 2026 paper on joint eviction and routing. All of them use exact token/block-prefix keys and report hit rate, throughput, latency, TTFT, fairness or load balance. None of them reports answer correctness, because exact prefix reuse is assumed to be lossless. (2) Answer-level semantic caches now measure wrong-answer rates directly: vCache (error-rate bound per cached prompt), GroundedCache (unsafe-served rate, with a gate on retrieved-evidence overlap) and SafeCache (unsafe-reuse target). These are single-cache, single-endpoint systems with no replicas, placement or load. (3) Approximate KV reuse (CacheBlend, EPIC, KVLink, Cache-Craft, KV-Repurpose, SAFE-KV, CacheRepair) and cross-model KV reuse (DroidSpeak, C2C, Semantic Cache Distillation, MoT, ElastKV) report quality-vs-speed trade-offs, typically F1/accuracy against full prefill, on one instance. None of them places or routes reuse units across replicas.

What is missing. I found no work that, in a multi-replica serving system, defines the reuse key from context identity (document/chunk IDs or a context embedding), uses that key for routing/placement, and measures realised cache hits, load concentration across replicas and per-context answer correctness together. I also found no measurement of "false locality" (affinity created by shared templates or system prompts rather than shared context). Both searches for this combination came back without a match.

Closest competitors, none of which covers all three axes: GroundedCache (arXiv 2605.27494) for context-anchored correctness gating (answer cache, one endpoint, no load axis); CacheRoute / DualMap / D²LPM for locality-vs-load (no correctness axis); Shared RAG-DCache (arXiv 2504.11765) for document-level KV sharing across instances (no correctness, no routing as described in the abstract); SemShareKV (Zhao and Mastorakis, IJCNLP-AACL Findings 2025) for LSH-keyed approximate KV reuse (one instance, no per-threshold accuracy curve visible in the abstract). SemShareKV shares an author with SEND and should be cited and distinguished explicitly.

Confidence: medium. Searches covered arXiv, ACL Anthology, USENIX, ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS pages and vendor docs, but 2026 preprints appear fast and many entries were checked only at abstract level, so a body-level overlap cannot be ruled out.

## 6. Joint correctness + locality + load in a serving system (novelty check)

No direct competitor found. Searches for routers or placement policies that report answer correctness alongside hit rate and load returned only locality/load papers (DualMap, CacheRoute, 2601.18999, TokenLake, Ray Serve/AMD/Dynamo docs) or correctness-only caches (vCache, GroundedCache, SafeCache, MeanCache). Pieces of the claim already exist and must be cited: context/evidence-anchored reuse gating (GroundedCache; MeanCache context chain), document-identity KV units (RAGCache, Cache-Craft, Shared RAG-DCache), model-partitioned reuse being necessary (DroidSpeak, ElastKV), and locality-vs-load hashing with rebalancing (DualMap, D²LPM).

## Points that cut against the hypotheses

- H1 "exact reuse is answer-safe": exact prefix reuse is not bit-identical in FP16 (arXiv 2604.15409), although measured accuracy did not drop there. Define correctness at answer level and report token-level divergence separately.
- H2 novelty: GroundedCache already uses retrieved-evidence overlap as a reuse condition, and SemShareKV already uses LSH keys for KV reuse. The new part has to be the routing/placement and the joint three-way measurement, not the context-anchored key itself.
- H3 "a context-aware key pushes the frontier outward": SAFE-KV reports that faithful multi-document repair re-encodes most of the context, so full prefill won in its setting; KV-Repurpose finds naive reuse fails on cross-document reasoning. The frontier may be near-degenerate for multi-hop tasks. CacheAttack argues the locality/collision-resistance conflict is inherent.
- Locality gains are workload-dependent: CacheRoute reports workloads where affinity gives little or no benefit.
- vCache's error bound is relative to the reference model, so "correct" needs a gold-answer definition to compare across these systems.
